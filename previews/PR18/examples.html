Worked examples ​

The examples/ folder holds two complete synthetic surveys and a set of short scripts for the everyday jobs.

A note on the data ​

Both surveys are synthetic. Every model, station position and gravity value in them is computed, not measured. They are placed on the Finnish national grid (ETRS-TM35FIN, EPSG:3067) around the GTK office in Otaniemi, Espoo, so that coordinates, ground elevations, anomaly amplitudes and error bars read like a real ground survey in Finnish Precambrian bedrock. No measured data is distributed with this package.

Run them ​

Each demo folder splits running from plotting. 00_run_inv_GN.jl and 00_run_inv_INR.jl invert the survey into a timestamped run_gn_<timestamp>/ or run_inr_<timestamp>/; nothing is plotted automatically:

bash
julia --project=. -t auto examples/demo1/00_run_inv_GN.jl
julia --project=. -t auto examples/demo1/00_run_inv_INR.jl

Then plot whatever runs you have, on demand:

bash
julia --project=. examples/demo1/01_plot_data.jl
julia --project=. examples/demo1/02_plot_model_XY.jl
julia --project=. examples/demo1/03_plot_model_XZ.jl
julia --project=. examples/demo1/04_plot_model_YZ.jl

Each plotting script finds the latest GN and INR run next to it and compares them against model.true; run only one algorithm and it plots just that one. The run and plot folders are gitignored, so you always reproduce them locally rather than reading someone else's output.

demo1 — blocky targets, dense grid ​

289 stations on a 100 m grid over 1.6 × 1.6 km of gently rolling coastal bedrock (ground 4–38 m). Two uniform ±200 kg/m³ blocks sit at 100–400 m depth: a mafic intrusion in granitic gneiss, and a clay-filled bedrock depression. The anomaly runs −0.75 to +0.73 mGal with 2 % + 5 µGal errors.

This is the classical setting, and Gauss–Newton takes it: correlation 0.66 against the INR's 0.63, reaching RMS 0.99 in a single step where the INR needs 281 epochs.

Both overshoot the mass and put it too deep — 15.8 Mt at 447 m and 12.6 Mt at 403 m against a true 8.8 Mt at 245 m. That is ordinary gravity depth ambiguity, not a failure of either method, and it is worth seeing on a case where you know the answer.

demo2 — gradational target, sparse survey ​

A basin 280 m deep whose fill grades from −200 kg/m³ at the surface to background at the floor, beside a 200 × 200 × 193 m block of +260 kg/m³ — a glacially scoured bedrock depression filled with soft sediment next to a mafic body, about 2 km north-west of the demo1 site. 150 scattered stations, with no access inside a 130 m clearing between the two bodies: 12 600 cells to constrain with 150 data.

Two things here are chosen to suit a coordinate network, and both are ordinary field conditions rather than tricks: the truth is a continuous function of position rather than a set of uniform blocks, and the survey is sparse and irregular.

Scored over the core against a true −3.05 Mt deficit at 52 m and +2.01 Mt excess at 147 m:

RMSiterationscorrelationdeficitits centroidexcessits centroid
Gauss–Newton0.9530.61−6.34 Mt189 m+2.52 Mt188 m
INR0.992570.81−2.89 Mt54 m+1.52 Mt119 m

Gauss–Newton buys the misfit with twice the mass it needs, buried below 180 m, and speckles the top layer where stations are sparse. The network returns a coherent bowl that shrinks with depth like the true one, and a compact block in place.

Gauss–Newton is not strawmanned: across stronger smallness, heavier smoothing, depth weighting and no weighting, its correlation stays between 0.41 and 0.65 and the basin centroid never comes shallower than 149 m.

The data are forward-modelled on a 2× refinement of the inversion mesh and given 2 % + 3 µGal noise, so the forward model sees structure no 40 m cell can hold — the true model on the inversion mesh only fits the data at RMS 2.14. That is deliberate: a synthetic where the truth fits perfectly is easier than any real survey.

The short scripts ​

Each has a settings block at the top to edit, and sensible defaults so it runs as-is.

ScriptWhat it does
make_mesh3D.jldesign a mesh, start model and covariance around a survey
run_fwd3D.jlpredict gravity from a model at a survey's stations
run_inv3D.jlrun an inversion and plot it; the control file picks the algorithm
plot_data3D.jlmap a survey, or observed/predicted/residual together
plot_model3D.jlopen a model in a slice, section or 3D viewer
bash
julia --project=. examples/make_mesh3D.jl examples/demo1/data.xyz
julia --project=. -t auto examples/run_inv3D.jl examples/demo1 InvCtrl.INR
julia --project=. examples/plot_model3D.jl examples/demo1/model.true xz

What ships, and what you make ​

Each demo folder holds inputs only — the files a six-file Invert3D call needs, plus model.true so you can score the result yourself. Everything a run produces (run folders, outputs, figures) is gitignored and regenerated when you run it.
